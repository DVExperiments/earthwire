    How Earthwire works

    Each dot is a news story placed where it happened. Stories come from the GDELT Project, which reads news from around the world in more than 100 languages every 15 minutes. Earthwire rebuilds the map every 30 minutes and keeps the last 24 hours. Daily totals are saved, so trends and unusual activity build up over time.

    Reading the map

    A solid dot is placed at a specific city or landmark. A ring is placed at the centre of a state or province, because the stories didn’t name anywhere more precise. Stories that only name a country aren’t drawn; open the country to see them. A pulsing dot has stories from the last hour. Bigger dots have more stories.

    Dots can be coloured by event type (what happened) or by topic (what the story is about). Switch with “Colour by” above the legend. Click a category anywhere to list its stories.

    Controls

    Drag to spin, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to zoom in. The pause button or the space bar stops and starts the slow spin; the globe also holds still while you’re looking at a place. The view button at the top (or H) hides the controls, then the side panel, for an uncluttered globe. Press / to search and Esc to go back to all news.

    How accurate is it?

    GDELT codes events, topics and locations automatically, so some stories will be in the wrong place or category, and a place can appear because it was mentioned rather than because something happened there. Treat the map as a guide to where the world’s attention is, then read the original reporting. Stories from non-English sources get an English summary headline, with the original headline and a translation link underneath.

    Sources

    News: GDELT Project. Population: WorldPop 2020 (CC BY 4.0). Weather: Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0). Air routes: OpenFlights (ODbL). Shipping lanes: Benden (2022), from a CIA map (CC BY 4.0). Antarctic stations: COMNAP. Borders, lakes and cities: Natural Earth (public domain). Borders are shown as Natural Earth draws them and don’t imply any position on disputed territory.

    Install it

    On a phone, use your browser’s “Add to Home Screen” option to install Earthwire as an app with its own icon.
