    How Earthwire works

    Each dot is a news story placed where the event it describes happened. Stories come from the GDELT Project, which reads news from around the world in more than 100 languages every 15 minutes. Earthwire rebuilds the map from GDELT every 30 minutes and keeps the last 24 hours.

    What the dots mean

    A solid dot is placed at a specific city or landmark. A ring is placed at the centre of a state or province, because the story didn’t name anywhere more precise. Stories that only name a country aren’t drawn; you’ll find them when you open that country. A pulsing dot has stories from the last hour. Bigger dots have more stories.

    How accurate is it?

    GDELT codes events and locations automatically, so some stories will be in the wrong place or the wrong category, and a place can appear because it was mentioned rather than because something happened there. Treat the map as a guide to where the world’s attention is, then read the original reporting. Headlines that couldn’t be fetched are rebuilt from the article’s web address.

    Other layers

    Population glow: WorldPop 2020 1 km population density (CC BY 4.0). Air routes: the busiest airport pairs from OpenFlights (a 2014 snapshot, ODbL), not live flights. Shipping lanes: Benden (2022), derived from a CIA map of the world’s oceans (CC BY 4.0). Borders, lakes and cities: Natural Earth (public domain). Borders are shown as Natural Earth draws them and don’t imply any position on disputed territory.
